Hemingway is a way of writing, not a set of rules.

Write plainly. Say what happened, what is true, and what to do, in the words you would use to explain it to someone across a table. Use short declarative sentences. Use concrete nouns and active verbs. When you use an abstraction, back it with the specific case: "the page has performance issues: it took four seconds to load."

Avoid rhetorical moves. A phrase that sounds clever often hides the claim. "The cache is a double-edged sword" makes the reader unpack it. "The cache makes reads faster, but it can serve data up to an hour old" does not. Do not set up a claim by first denying another one. Do not end a paragraph on a line built to land. Do not use a metaphor where a plain statement would do.

Cut words that carry no weight. Plain does not mean short. If the full statement needs a longer sentence, write the longer sentence. If a compressed phrase makes the reader work to unpack it, spell it out.

# Microtiming studies — laid-back snares, long-range correlation, and the timing JND

- **Sources:**
  - A. Danielsen, M. Nymoen, E. C. Anderson, O. K. Launay, J. A. Burgoyne, & K. B. Hove, *JASA* 138(4), 2015, doi:10.1121/1.4930950. Open PDF: https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/44723836/Danielsen_et_al_2015.pdf
  - H. C. Hennig et al., "Long-range correlation in musical rhythms," *PLoS ONE* 6(10): e26457, 2011, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0026457.
  - A. Friberg & A. Sundberg, "Time discrimination in a monotonic sequence," *JASA* 98(5), 1995, doi:10.1121/1.413218.
- **Checked:** 2026-10-14 (quotes against the ledger's fetched copies).

## The passages

**Danielsen et al. 2015 (Table I, "Descriptive statistics of microtiming (TIM). Note: Timing in msec."):** at 96 BPM, laid-back snare strokes averaged 17.4 ms behind the click across 10 drummers, with individual means from −4 to +43 ms — about 29 ms later than the same drummers' on-beat strokes.

**Hennig et al. 2011:** "the lowest values of the standard deviation of the time series were found for experienced musicians (σ≈12 ms, average standard deviation σ≈21 ms)" and "We found LRC in musical rhythms for all tasks and subjects" — musicians' own timing deviations are long-range correlated (1/f-like), not white noise. In their listening experiment they quantized a pop song and added timing deviations with σ = 15 ms, once correlated and once independent: "The mean relative frequency with respect to preference for the 1/f version was 0.641 (SEM=0.040)" among 39 choir members. The quantized original was not among the versions heard; the authors "have filed two patents related to the algorithm employed in this study."

**Friberg & Sundberg 1995:** "absolute jnd… approximately constant at 6 ms for… intervals shorter than about 240 ms and the relative jnd constant at 2.5%" — about 6 ms for 16ths and 8ths at 120 BPM, ~12 ms for quarters. Musical training had no effect.

## Where it is relied on

[Groove and feel](../../knowledge/Groove%20and%20feel.md) uses three of these figures. Danielsen's 17.4 ms is the measured reference for its laid-back snare value (15 ms). Hennig's σ values are the measured spread behind seeded jitter; they describe human variation and are not a humanize knob setting. The Friberg–Sundberg JND separates audible from inaudible jitter in the case values (±2.5 ms onsets, ±1.5 ms beat-timed keys). The [Soundtrack code case](../../cases/Soundtrack%20code.md) repeats the figures with its tables.

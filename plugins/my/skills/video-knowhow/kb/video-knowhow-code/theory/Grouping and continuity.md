# Grouping and continuity across frames

In a still frame, shared alignment, proximity, the gaps between elements, and repeated form can make separate elements read as one group. Static design describes grouping and coherence as coming from relationships: shared axes, baselines, and governing edges; relative gaps; repeated form and other properties that stay the same; and the scale of neighboring elements. Geometry is the starting point because its choices can be explained. Keep an optical correction or an intentional break only when you can name what it helps the viewer read. Moving pictures add a second question: after the frame changes, which group, object, or idea does the viewer take to be the same one?

## Three kinds of continuity

**Object continuity** follows one recognizable thing as its location, scale, shape, or appearance changes. **Structural continuity** keeps a relationship in place, such as a title staying anchored to the character it names or a menu staying in the same region of the screen. **Conceptual continuity** links separate images through a repeated color, silhouette, word, sound, or action. These are categories for describing a design, and one transition may use several at once.

Heer and Robertson found that animated transitions between related charts can help viewers track what changed, and they warn that morphing one thing into an unrelated thing can imply a relation that does not exist. Material Design also uses motion to show how interface elements and levels relate. The evidence is strongest for charts and interfaces, so check how it applies in the actual sequence of a trailer or explainer. [Heer and Robertson](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/communication/HeerRobertson2007.pdf), [Material motion principles](https://m2.material.io/design/motion/understanding-motion/)

## Continuity can help or mislead

A code renderer can keep object IDs stable across scenes, hold anchor positions, or interpolate geometry. That makes seamless movement cheap, and a smooth move is not always truthful. A match cut can say "these states correspond," and a hard cut can say "this is a different situation." When a transformation implies that two things are the same object, that one caused the other, or that they share a location, the story should support that reading.

Grouping can also change within a shot. Two objects that move together may look related even when they are far apart, and a label that travels apart from its subject may break an association that was clear a moment before. The path, timing, and relative motion of elements are therefore part of the composition and get designed along with the layout.

To inspect continuity, compare recognizable features just before and after a boundary, then watch the change at normal speed. Ask whether a viewer can tell what stayed the same, what changed, and whether the implied relationship is the one you meant.

# Case: FOXTALE pixel-game trailer

This page records a session from the earlier production. The trailer's media is not preserved, and the assistant that made its score could not listen to it. It is a specimen of how such a film was made and checked.

**Result.** The chatlog records a completed 48-second, 1920×1080, 30 fps H.264/AAC trailer, at the time named `ax-day/foxtale/foxtale_trailer.mp4`. A separate, self-contained `foxtale.html` was built after the original `index.html` failed when opened by double click. These are past outputs named in the chatlog. The raw chatlog file stays with John; the [chatlog index](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md) ships with the package and describes each cited record. [C-1112](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1112), [C-1126](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1126), [C-1234](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1234), [C-1253](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1253), [C-1319](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1319)

## Creative and technical choice

John asked for an Undertale-style promotional video and left the choice of technology to the assistant. [C-869](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-869) The assistant chose a trailer for a fictional game, “FOXTALE”: pixel art, dialogue boxes, a title card, exploration, a menu and a bullet-avoidance fight steered by a heart, a mercy choice, and a closing line. The assistant says it made new characters and music and copied no Undertale assets. The package has not independently audited that originality or the claimed Galmuri font license. [C-1146](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1146)

Canvas 2D draws a 480×270 logical game frame and scales it 4× without smoothing. Each sprite row is a string, and symmetrical sprites are mirrored from a half-width pattern. A row-width check caught one malformed sprite, which was fixed. A fox illustration that already existed became an 11-color portrait. That shows a useful split: image material can supply detailed visual character while code supplies dialogue, staging, and motion. The chatlog leaves it uncertain where the original illustration came from. [C-926](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-926), [C-942](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-942), [C-953](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-953), [C-1146](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1146), [C-1332](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1332)

The shared 120 BPM, 96-beat timeline sets when each character of dialogue appears and the sound blip that goes with it. It also sets the menu moves, three attack patterns (falling rows with gaps, side bolts, then closing rings), and the positions of the protagonist's heart. A planned collision at beat 69.5 drops HP to 14, and a mercy resolution follows. The fight is choreographed and no player controls it: the collision, the HP, and the mercy are scripted events. [C-899](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-899), [C-1146](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1146)

## Music and sound as part of the scene

The 44.1 kHz score is generated in JavaScript from pulse waves, a quantized triangle wave, and shift-register noise. A note-string helper places melodies on eighth-note units. The source divides the music by what each part does in the story: a sparse A-minor introduction, a title theme, C-major exploration, an encounter break, a driving E-minor battle over Em–D–C–B harmony, a softer passage for the mercy choice and tagline, and a fuller closing reprise. Three pitches of text voice stand for the narrator, the system, and NPCs, and the renderer reported 187 timed blips. There are also sound effects for the menu, projectiles, damage, the logo, and sparkles. The code applies a low-pass filter, soft clipping, a fade, and normalization. [C-899](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-899), [C-1081](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1081), [C-1112](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1112), [C-1549](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1549)

## Review and delivery

The assistant exported stills, read contact sheets, and changed the floating fox, the sprite layering, the combat trajectory, and the small Korean text at the end. The source then reports a 1,440-frame render, and an ffmpeg probe shows a 48-second file with H.264 video and AAC audio. The assistant reports checking the collision and the gap in the rings frame by frame. It could measure audio levels but could not listen to the tune. [C-1043](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1043), [C-1070](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1070), [C-1112](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1112), [C-1126](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1126), [C-1131](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1131), [C-1146](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1146)

The MP4 rendered through a local server, but opening the source `index.html` through `file://` showed a black frame. A probe reproduced a cross-origin `getImageData` SecurityError: reading pixels from an external fox image had tainted the Canvas. The fix inlined the scripts, fonts, image, and AAC audio into `foxtale.html`, and the preview takes the playing audio's `currentTime` as its picture clock. A browser probe then reported audio running at 3.94 seconds with no page errors, and the standalone page was opened. Playback starts only after a click on the page. [C-1155](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1155), [C-1234](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1234), [C-1253](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1253), [C-1264](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1264), [C-1286](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1286), [C-1319](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1319)

**Interpretation:** with a small, fixed set of pixel shapes and colors, much of the trailer can be written down as coordinates, palettes, text, and timed rules. Detailed character illustration and subtle acting are separate art problems, and the assistant judged its hand-coded sprites to be prototype quality. A delivery test has to open the file the way the viewer will, because a successful render through a local server did not catch the `file://` failure. [C-1146](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1146), [C-1234](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1234), [C-1332](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md#c-1332)

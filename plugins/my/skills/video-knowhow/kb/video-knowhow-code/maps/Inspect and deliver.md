# Inspect and deliver

The scene code, the browser preview, the captured frames, the encoded file, and what the viewer plays back can all differ. Inspect the form that the decision depends on. [Verification](../knowledge/Verification.md) helps you choose checks; [capture and encoding](../knowledge/Capture%20and%20encoding.md) covers recording frames; [delivery and color](../knowledge/Delivery%20and%20color.md) covers the path to the viewer's screen.

Check the moments most likely to change your judgment: entry and exit frames, scene boundaries, fast motion, text at actual size, loudness changes, and the conditions where assets load or fail to load. [Mastering and delivery](../knowledge/Mastering%20and%20delivery.md) measures loudness and true peak on the encoded file, and [music under voice-over](../knowledge/Music%20under%20voice-over.md) checks that narration stays clear over the music. [Accessibility and perception](../knowledge/Accessibility%20and%20perception.md) adds viewing conditions that a render under nominal settings can miss. A warning card that says where flashing is strongest has to [follow the flash check](../knowledge/Accessibility%20and%20perception.md#flash-warnings-that-follow-the-flash-check) of the delivered file after every picture change. If the scene code has to survive seeking or repeated capture, read [deterministic rendering](../knowledge/Deterministic%20rendering.md) and [audiovisual synchronization](../knowledge/Audiovisual%20synchronization.md).

The [FOXTALE](../cases/FOXTALE.md) case includes a reported problem with preview and delivery. Use the [chatlog index](../sources/Chatlog%20index.md) to tell the observed failure apart from the proposed fix.

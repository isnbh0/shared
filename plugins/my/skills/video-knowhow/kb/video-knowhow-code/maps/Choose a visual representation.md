# Choose a visual representation

Start with the structure the image depends on. Does it need crisp text, layered shapes, per-pixel effects, spatial lighting, hand-drawn irregularity, or photographic detail? [Representation models](../knowledge/Representation%20models.md) compares what each medium can express and how easily you can revise it.

- For text, interfaces, scalable geometry, and layout, read [raster, vector, and DOM](../knowledge/Raster%20vector%20and%20DOM.md) and [typography in motion](../knowledge/Typography%20in%20motion.md).
- For fields of color, distortion, particles, lighting, or spatial form, read [shaders and 3D](../knowledge/Shaders%20and%203D.md).
- For an intentionally low-resolution visual grammar, read [pixel art and stylization](../knowledge/Pixel%20art%20and%20stylization.md).

[Visual hierarchy and composition](../knowledge/Visual%20hierarchy%20and%20composition.md) covers what the viewer should notice and when. [Accessibility and perception](../knowledge/Accessibility%20and%20perception.md) helps you test whether the form you chose stays readable and comfortable to watch in the viewing conditions you expect.

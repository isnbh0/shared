# Video know-how: craft

Make each visible or audible choice serve its scene, then make the scenes serve the piece. This shelf follows a choice from a single motion control up through the component it shapes, the scene and sequence around it, the direction of the whole piece, and the engineering that delivers it. It is for human makers and coding agents producing explainers, openers, product films, and data stories. It is the craft shelf of the video know-how package; its sibling, [Video know-how: code](../video-knowhow-code/Home.md), is the code shelf, and both folders share the `video-knowhow` prefix.

The package relies on the code shelf's externally documented practice and on what was learned from SLASH_MY, the skills hype film, the one production John endorses. The craft generalizations are informed interpretation, drawn from that production and its comparison runs. John directed SLASH_MY's craft through briefs and reviews; these generalizations came afterward and have yet to guide a production of their own. [Content lineage](../video-knowhow-code/sources/Content%20lineage.md) records the source behind each page.

## Choose a starting point

1. **Learn step by step**: [the scopes map](maps/Scopes.md) moves from attention and individual controls to scenes, sequences, and whole pieces. Start with [a typographic event](knowledge/Typographic%20events.md) to see how layout, movement, surface, and sound interact.
2. **Make a deliverable**: [make](maps/Make.md) connects audience and medium to Awesome AI Motion's "timed recipes", the code shelf's recipes, and review.
3. **Fix a weak passage**: [diagnose](maps/Diagnose.md) offers candidate causes, alternatives, and inspection questions for work that is unreadable, repetitive, disconnected, weightless, thin, noisy, or emotionally flat. [Convergence](knowledge/Convergence.md) runs the rounds that close a revision.
4. **Find a technique**: the shelf draws on an upstream motion-design reference collection, [gongnyang's Awesome AI Motion](https://github.com/gongnyang/awesome-ai-motion), whose ~637 technique cards, family maps, 11 "timed recipes", and seven deliverable routes cover easing, reveals, transitions, data graphics, and camera work. This shelf curates the parts its own cases rely on. Use the full upstream collection for study and adaptation.
5. **Read the roundup**: the [motion production roundup](roundup/motion-production-roundup.md) gathers the shelf's main points on one page.
6. **Study a production**: [cases](maps/Cases.md) connects SLASH_MY, Stage intro and its AX DAY reconstruction, FOXTALE, and the comparison runs to their evidence and source.

## Follow one choice through the piece

- [Jitter to repair](cases/Jitter%20to%20repair.md): sharp instability becomes evidence of a defect, then gives way to a repair the viewer can follow.
- [Page to larger context](cases/Page%20to%20larger%20context.md): a camera move takes the same document into a graph.
- [Anticipation to title](cases/Anticipation%20to%20title.md): an opening request sets up the result that the title later delivers.

You can read each path upward, from implementation to purpose, or downward, from direction to controls. A difficult shader may change only one surface, and a simple cut may deliver the central reversal.

## Keep the reference shelves close

The craft generalizations here draw on two reference collections that stay in John's workspace. The motion reference is a generated mirror of the public [Awesome AI Motion](https://github.com/gongnyang/awesome-ai-motion) repository at a recorded snapshot: focused motion decision notes, technique cards with their upstream implementations, "recipes", and a source glossary. Where a page here leans on one of its cards, it summarizes the relied-on claim inline, and the card's full implementation stays upstream. The static-design grounding (relational visual weight, grouping, typographic roles, and color systems) comes from John's own design collection, and its principles are written out where this shelf uses them. [The code shelf](../video-knowhow-code/Home.md) owns representation, reusable operations, engineering, and the original cases.

The craft shelf adds connections and worked explanations of craft. Numerical settings belong to the examples they come from. SLASH_MY is John's strongest reported reference here, and [its case](cases/SLASH_MY.md) reads the film's craft decisions one by one.

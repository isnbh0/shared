## Read downward from intention

To make a result feel earned, first make clear that it is missing. Then point the build at it: here the particles share the title's target geometry before the drop. Use the pause and the impact to mark the arrival, then let the solid letters and the supporting information take over. The [worked event](../knowledge/Typographic%20events.md) gives the exact order, beats, seconds, layout dependencies, compositor behavior, and sound roles.

## Read upward from an effect

Start from the particle assembly and ask what it forms and why that form matters. Then ask whether the arrival answers a question the scene has raised, whether the scenes before it prepare it, and whether the ending delivers on the opening premise. If the particles only resolve into a generic slogan, the mechanism by itself pays nothing off. A restrained mask reveal could answer the same request in a quieter direction.

**Evaluate**: at normal playback speed, can the viewer connect the final title with the opening request? Can they read the event facts after the impact? Does the score leave space for that reading?

The reconstruction's sound analysis reports that John found the AX DAY reconstruction more polished than A/B, with music that sounded less MIDI-like. The two pieces differed in brief, frame rate, and loudness, so the result is one comparison between those two particular pieces. This case is a reading of the source. SLASH_MY is John's more recent and strongest reference, and [its case](SLASH_MY.md) states the separate limits of that feedback.

# Two soundtracks on one ladder

This case compares two soundtracks as a ladder: a list of sound choices, called rungs, in which each rung builds on the ones below it. The AX DAY reconstruction and SLASH_MY score their pictures with the same kind of tools: sound synthesized in code at 120 BPM, a timeline shared with the picture, and a silence before a drop. Both films share the lower rungs, and SLASH_MY adds several more above them. Each rung links to the code shelf page that explains its mechanism. The ladder orders the choices for study and adaptation. It ranks the two films only by John's judgment of each whole film, and no listening comparison has isolated any rung. Both are electronic, beat-driven promotional scores, so the ladder is a rule of thumb for scoring pieces of that kind.

## Version and availability

The AX DAY reconstruction was recovered from a chatlog. Its original source and MP4 are unavailable, and its reproduction notes stay with the specimen in John's workspace, where its sound was checked for presence and decoding. The reconstruction's snapshot is `e5ae340d`, the last commit touching its `audio.js`, `timeline.js`, and `index.html`; these files are identical at the `32dec413` snapshot used by [the anticipation case](Anticipation%20to%20title.md).

SLASH_MY's audio is read at `fd543f2a`, and [the SLASH_MY case](SLASH_MY.md) reads its picture at `32dec413`. The two snapshots bracket a superseded mix fix: the picture snapshot has the beat-72 clip step in its whole-mix form, which the audio snapshot replaces with a band-only dip. Each case's values are true of the snapshot it names. On 2026-10-02 John called SLASH_MY "the most polished/convincing" (human feedback; the [SLASH_MY case](SLASH_MY.md#what-this-case-is) states what that verdict covers).

Everything below comes from source inspection unless it is labeled otherwise. Figures quoted from commits are the production's reported checks.

The line references, parameter tables, and the commits' measurements for both scores are in the [Soundtrack code case](../../video-knowhow-code/cases/Soundtrack%20code.md).

The AX DAY reconstruction's master is `tanh(1.1x)`, a linear fade from beat 46, and normalization to 0.89 of peak, written as 16-bit. The reconstruction's sound analysis reports its decoded render at about −14.3 LUFS integrated. SLASH_MY adds TPDF dither of 2 LSB peak-to-peak wherever it reduces word length (`f7f9d199`: `audio.js` `writeWav` for the 24-bit WAVs, `render.js` `pcm16` for the encoder's 16-bit input); the AX DAY reconstruction rounds to 16 bits without dither. The only external support for any rung is what the [audio source ledger](../../video-knowhow-code/sources/Audio%20sources.md) records. The ledger also records that syncing sound to the picture's beat grid, silence before a drop, pre-roll whooshes and risers, layered impacts, and delay throws are common practice with no authoritative source found.

## Sounds that lead the picture

Both soundtracks place a cue so that its audible sync landmark lands on the picture's landmark, wherever that puts the start of the sound. The AX DAY reconstruction sets its whoosh lead by hand in two files and runs its riser on shared beat constants. In SLASH_MY the picture defines more of the sync landmarks, such as scene exits and the inhale before 104, and the score reads them. The cue table, the mechanisms, and the strike carve's reported look-ahead check are in [sound and picture on one beat grid](../../video-knowhow-code/knowledge/Audiovisual%20synchronization.md#sound-and-picture-on-one-beat-grid). First decide what the sound anticipates in the [typographic event](../knowledge/Typographic%20events.md) or scene, then keep that relationship through export.

## What remains unresolved

The rungs come from source inspection, and which of them John heard is the open question. A first answer would come from reverting one rung at a time in SLASH_MY, blind and loudness-matched, with the picture held fixed: swing straightened, carves removed, or the rooms collapsed to one hall. [D-007](Comparison%20runs.md) sets the expectation. When a picture onset was changed, the two versions looked equivalent at playback speed, so a sound rung may likewise need a dedicated listen before its effect is heard.

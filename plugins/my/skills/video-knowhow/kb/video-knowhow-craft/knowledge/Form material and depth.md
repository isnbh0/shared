# Form, material, and depth

When an object feels thin or unfinished, look at its outline, its surface, and its spatial relationships separately before adding treatments. Repairing a path can remove a defect and still leave an unconvincing concept in place. The [D-series](../cases/Comparison%20runs.md), as it developed, shows this difference, and it does not settle a general recipe for quality.

## Read the object in layers

**Form**: inspect silhouette, proportion, joins, and placement. Does the object have a recognizable role at thumbnail size? A large central object takes much of the attention the piece has to spend, and delicate line work cannot supply a reason to watch it if the object lacks one.

**Material**: inspect which surfaces carry texture and how light changes across them. Grain drawn over a whole composite also crosses the object in its foreground. Moving the grain behind the object makes the object's face smoother, but it changes how the object relates to the world around it. Neither placement is inherently more finished.

**Depth**: inspect occlusion, inner walls, gradients, and movement together. A dark disk inside a ring can read as a flat insert. An offset aperture clipped inside the front opening can expose an inner wall and suggest a recess behind the opening. It does not supply the rest of a coherent 3D surface or justify orbiting the camera around unseen geometry.

These inspection strategies are editorial. They come from the D-003 run notes, summarized in [the comparison runs](../cases/Comparison%20runs.md), at snapshot `32dec413`. The run notes record the matched stills, the variant HTML, and the observations, in text and in contact sheets kept with the run.

## What the controlled stills support

At 10 and 18 seconds, **J** aligns neighboring contour boundaries and removes the radial connector that appeared because a closed annular path was stroked as one loop. It keeps the original outside and inside boundaries. This repairs the joins, but it changes more than one drawing defect at once, so the benefit cannot be assigned to any single join.

**G** keeps J's form and moves the grain behind it. The record describes a smoother face and clearer separation between bands at thumbnail size. That is a change in how readable the surface is, and it may cost some of the object's integration into the textured film around it.

**D** keeps G's front outline and adds a clipped dark aperture shifted 13 px right and 43 px down, which reveals an inner wall. The record describes a clearer recess in plain frames but only subtle depth at thumbnail size. The screen-space gradients and repeated front contours remain. Plain captures leave out parts of the compositor, so they must not be compared as if they were matched finished frames.

John tentatively preferred J in both rows, then questioned the form itself. No D-003 film was rendered, and all three of D-004's later sketches were rejected. These findings do not support recommending G or D as proven improvements, and they do not show that rings are a poor concept in themselves.

## Choose the next comparison

If a seam is visibly broken, fix the seam and keep the silhouette and framing. If the form is ambiguous, compare a clearer arrangement of light and occlusion before also changing the texture. If the object is simply uninteresting for the premise, sketch a different visual relationship before polishing its material. A flat graphic can be the right alternative when hierarchy or exact comparison matters more than depth.

Keep titles and quantitative marks off distorting surfaces when reading them requires stability. Grain, light, and distortion belong in the frame only while the required text and values stay legible, so a treatment that changes letterforms or a real product screen goes on a separate background layer. [Representation models](../../video-knowhow-code/knowledge/Representation%20models.md) and [shaders and 3D](../../video-knowhow-code/knowledge/Shaders%20and%203D.md) describe the implementation choices. Two upstream mechanisms recur across the record: a fine animated grain layer, updated at a low frame rate, for film texture; and a tilted band of light passed once through the glyphs for a metallic sheen.

**Evaluate**: compare matched finished stills at full size and at thumbnail size, then test a promising treatment in motion. Does the viewer see a more coherent object, or only more detail? Does the object serve the [direction](Direction%20and%20critique.md)?

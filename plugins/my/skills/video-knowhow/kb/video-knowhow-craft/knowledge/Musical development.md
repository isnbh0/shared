# Musical development

Plan the phrase, melody, harmony, groove, and dramatic development apart from how the instruments are built and how the mix is processed. Both meet in the soundtrack, but a new reverb cannot supply a missing phrase, and a good melody can still be masked by its arrangement. [Sound production](Sound%20production.md) covers the instrument and mix decisions.

## Compose the relationships

**Phrase**: find a beginning, a continuation, an arrival, and the space after it. Place a visual reveal where the phrase can give it weight. A sequence that keeps adding subdivisions never releases. The AX DAY reconstruction's countdown, accelerating roll, gap, and sustained title chord form a phrase of this kind, supported by its source; see [anticipation to title](../cases/Anticipation%20to%20title.md).

**Melody and motif**: choose a recognizable contour or rhythmic cell, then decide what changes when it comes back. Repetition sets up its identity. Augmentation can stretch a familiar cell into a calmer passage, and a change of register or an answering phrase can shift its role. These are compositional options to audition; a score does not need all of them.

**Harmony**: decide whether a section should continue, depart, suspend, or return. A brighter chord extension can change the color without creating a new tonal destination. The C comparison, summarized in [the comparison runs](../cases/Comparison%20runs.md), records F major, F major seventh, and F major ninth across C's later sections, against the AX DAY reconstruction's Am–F–C–G cycle, which returns at its reveal. The reading that the AX DAY reconstruction gives a clearer arrival is a hypothesis that agrees with John's report, offered for audition.

**Groove**: define the pulse and where attacks fall around it. In the same comparison, C's tunnel bass attacks land with its kicks, while the AX DAY reconstruction's bass sits offbeat. Ducking lowers the melodic level under a kick, so placement and mix interact: an attack on the kick may lose the articulation that was meant to push the phrase forward. Landing together can also be intentional. Inspect the actual attacks and listen before moving notes.

**Development**: map which musical role changes with the story. Adding instruments raises density, but development can also mean removing a pulse, changing the motif's duration, changing the harmony, or letting an answer replace a question. The [FOXTALE case](../../video-knowhow-code/cases/FOXTALE.md) reports exploration, encounter, battle, mercy, and reprise, each with a matching musical section. Its local media and matching implementation are unavailable, so treat the written account as reported case evidence.

## Worked direction connection in SLASH_MY

At the [SLASH_MY snapshot](../cases/SLASH_MY.md), the brief describes a hook with a 3-3-2-3-3-2 rhythm and an E–E–G head. It starts as the typing rhythm of the complaint, becomes a brand pluck, changes with each section's groove, slows into the top voice of the writing section, and returns as the reveal's lead. The brief pairs the calmer paper passage with half-time and swing, and then has the reveal's A-major arrival answer the arc that came before.

The part that transfers to other work is **recognizable material taking on a new dramatic role**. The particular pitches, the paper palette, and the major-mode payoff belong to SLASH_MY. In a reflective documentary, the same motif could come back quieter and unresolved. In a data story, a steady pulse with fewer melodic changes might keep the narration clear.

## A score map worth writing

For each scene, write down the audience's new information, the phrase's role, the pulse or groove, how the motif is treated, the harmonic destination, and what should be in the foreground. Then mark the visual sync landmark that belongs with the musical event: onset, arrival, pause, or release. A story beat does not automatically fall on a quarter note. When a spoken explanation sets the timing, narration comes first: line up each visual change with the moment the speaker names the object or states the relationship it explains, and keep entrance, selection, and arrival as separate timestamps.

**Evaluate**: listen across section boundaries and through the whole piece at a steady level. Can the listener follow the pulse and recognize a return? Does a visual climax arrive after the music has already spent its surprise? Before crediting timbre with an improvement, compare a change in phrasing with the instrument sounds held fixed.

The C2 timing ablation, summarized in [the comparison runs](../cases/Comparison%20runs.md), removed one extra kick and kept the rest of the score; John rated both versions bad. Fixing a collision is one repair inside a larger musical judgment.

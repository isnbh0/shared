# Make a deliverable

Start with the audience's task and the evidence the piece must show. Recipe times are examples to adapt once the actual words, values, assets, and viewing conditions are known.

| Deliverable | Plan and construct | Inspect before delivery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opener | [Direction](../knowledge/Direction%20and%20critique.md) → [meaningful reveal](../patterns/Meaningful%20reveal.md) → an opening-hook arrangement — a question or promise in the first seconds that later scenes answer — or the [event announcement recipe](../../video-knowhow-code/recipes/Event%20announcement%20opener.md). Study [the AX DAY reconstruction](../cases/Anticipation%20to%20title.md) for an earned result. | Can a first-time viewer name the subject? Are event facts readable after the impact? Does the score's arrival leave a hold? |
| Explainer | Reveal the process in steps while keeping earlier context readable, so the current step can be explained against what came before → the [diagram recipe](../../video-knowhow-code/recipes/Diagram%20explainer.md) → schedule each visual change against the narration's meaning points (when the speaker names an object or states a result). | Can the learner explain the relationship? Label illustrative AI values; keep narration intelligible over the [music under it](../../video-knowhow-code/knowledge/Music%20under%20voice-over.md) and essential information available without sound. |
| Product film | Connect a customer problem to a value promise and visible evidence, then show the proof → [defect to repair](../patterns/Defect%20to%20repair.md) → a product demo that walks input, action, result, and verification in order. Use [SLASH_MY](../cases/SLASH_MY.md) for varied mechanisms under one direction. | Does a visible action actually support the promised benefit? Distinguish recorded, reconstructed, and simulated behavior; disclose consequential retiming. |
| Data story | [Focused comparison](../patterns/Focused%20comparison.md) → a data-story arrangement that completes the final explanatory graphic first (claim, marks, exact values, units, source) and moves only when the transformation itself matters → the [animated data recipe](../../video-knowhow-code/recipes/Animated%20data%20comparison.md). | Check units, reference baseline, denominator, stable identity, and source. Can both endpoints be compared after movement stops? |

## Adapt to the medium

The upstream reference collection keeps a route for each deliverable. The main adaptation in each:

- **Video editing**: divide the script into meaning units, finalize narration first, and preserve input–action–result–verification when a demonstration is cut; label compressed waiting where it affects a product claim.
- **Shorts**: the upstream medium map pairs the vertical feed with stable captions and framing that suits the format. The default is a layout designed for the vertical frame itself; shrinking a desktop layout into it is a trade to judge on your own material. Give the actual words enough completed-state time. Treat a first-second hook as a proposal to test.
- **Intro and promo**: connect promise to visible evidence; make a demonstration's simulated-or-measured status clear.
- **Infographic**: complete the final graphic first (claim, exact values, units, source), keep identifiers and axes stable during motion, and retain a final paired display for exact comparison.
- **Decks and sites**: decide who controls progress — presenter, scroll, or explicit steps — and preserve readable states at actual navigation landing points.
- **Newsletter**: make the initial still and nearby text informative if motion does not play; link out for explanations needing more reading time.
- **Thumbnail**: compose a complete, truthful still; do not pick an incomplete reveal frame.

The upstream collection's "recipe map" keeps all 11 timed arrangements, and its medium map the source-specific adaptations. Both are reference records from [Awesome AI Motion](https://github.com/gongnyang/awesome-ai-motion). Their numbers belong to their example timelines; adapt them to your actual copy.

# Defect to repair

Show the problem, show the action that fixes it, then hold a stable state where the viewer can understand the result. The object itself has to change in the way the repair claims.

## Build three states

**Defect**: pick one specific failed relationship, such as labels that have come loose from their anchors or a stale dependency. Keep enough order that the viewer still recognizes the system as it should work. Noise with no structure can hide the problem it was meant to show.

**Repair**: decide what acts on what. In SLASH_MY's visual-grounding scene, the event script names each step of the defect and the fix, including `CRACK`, `REFLOW`, `SLAM`, `FUSE`, and `SNAP`. A strike can mark the switch from defect to repair while the prop realigns, reconnects, or updates. Stop the old instability at that switch, and show the completed result only after the action that causes it, unless the result is meant to appear early. SLASH_MY's session records log one failure of this rule, a landed state shown about 42 ms before its beat; the [jitter path](../cases/Jitter%20to%20repair.md) gives the fix and the seam document's rule, "no hit shows before its beat", where "hit" means a picture event at the beat.

**Result**: keep the same objects and make the correction visible. Hold it long enough to inspect. The payoff wording names the consequence, but it should not be the only evidence that anything improved.

The [SLASH_MY jitter path](../cases/Jitter%20to%20repair.md) connects a stepped pain clock, seeded offsets, the strike, smooth time, and prop changes that differ with each repair mechanism. SLASH_MY's visual-grounding scene code describes a guide that cracks into word underlines and a layout that reflows wrong while the checks keep passing. SLASH_MY's scene API changed as the implementation evolved, and the path records the snapshot that was inspected. The general pattern on this page is an editorial interpretation of how the production responded.

## Adapt without copying the metaphor

Jitter can communicate instability, but a defect of meaning may read more clearly as a wrong label, a missing connection, or a misalignment. The jitter path gives a chart example: a wrong value can be perfectly stable, so jitter may mislead there.

In a factual product demo, show the real state or say what the illustration is: a recording, a faithful reconstruction, or a simulated example. If you cut out waiting and that changes how fast the product seems, say so. As a rule of thumb, a repair animation shows how the fix works. A claim about product speed comes from a recording of the product itself.

In a calmer piece, replace SLASH_MY's strike with a selection highlight and a precise state change, and keep the link between problem and solution. For a problem that is still unsolved, a [focused comparison](Focused%20comparison.md) may be more honest than a completed repair.

**Evaluate**: hide the payoff wording in a review copy. Can someone still tell what was repaired? Can the defect and the result be compared? With the sound muted, does the chain of cause and effect hold? Then play the full mix and check whether the strike accents the repair without masking the payoff wording during its reading time.

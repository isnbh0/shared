# Meaningful reveal

Use a reveal when finding out the result changes what the viewer understands or expects. Set up a question or a relationship with a piece missing, give the audience enough evidence to anticipate a consequence, then show the result and leave time to read it. A mask or a particle assembly supplies the mechanism; the scenes before it supply the reason to care.

## Construct the relationship

1. Establish what is missing: an output, an identity, a location, an explanation, or a consequence.
2. Keep a recognizable carrier through the preparation. It might be the material that forms the title, a repeated phrase, or the object whose context will expand.
3. Choose the main sync landmark. Align its motion and sound there, and leave whatever recovery time the eye needs before reading.
4. Hold the complete result, including qualifications and supporting information.

In [the AX DAY reconstruction](../cases/Anticipation%20to%20title.md), the opening request asks for an event video and names the event, and the reveal answers the request with the result, the completed title. The [typographic walkthrough](../knowledge/Typographic%20events.md) reads the timing from source: the drop at beat 32 (16 s) brings the particle burst, the flash, and the kick; the glitch decays over beats 32–32.7; and the solid letters fade in over beats 32.6–34.2, so most reading happens after the strongest displacement. In [SLASH_MY](../cases/SLASH_MY.md), the brief withholds the full identity and later combines John's caret and the retained word “my” into the lockup. The two pair setup and payoff in different ways. Neither requires the title to be secret: in the AX DAY reconstruction the event name is known from the start, and what the production withholds is its completed form.

## Variations and tradeoffs

A quiet uncovering can invite inspection; a large impact can punctuate an expectation the earlier scenes built. In a product demo, showing the result early may help the viewer understand the mechanism that follows. In an explainer, revealing the process in steps while earlier context stays readable is usually more useful than hiding the answer. Begin with the overall input, process, and output, then show each detail with its label nearby, and leave earlier steps quiet but available for comparison. Withholding for too long can become delay when the viewer has no useful question to pursue.

Choose the relationship first, then the mechanism that assembles the result. A mask reveal raises the title into clipped windows one line at a time, and it works best when the entrance should be quiet and the geometry is shared. Scattered particles that gather into an image's shape and color suit a build that should visibly become the word. In the AX DAY reconstruction, particles move toward sampled glyph targets from beat 29.5, and the walkthrough reads this as the material starting to belong to the word before it turns solid. A proportional scale change from a stated anchor shows the direction and amount of growth clearly. The typographic walkthrough explains how particles, solid type, impact, and hold interact in a recorded implementation.

**Evaluate**: what expectation does the reveal fulfill or revise? At the reveal's strongest frame, can the viewer read the result? After the impact subsides, does the held image say more than the preparation did? Watch the whole setup as well as the reveal excerpt.

# Motion production: a Tractatus roundup

**Global glossary.** Terms: [Piece](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#piece), [Scene](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#scene), [Sequence](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#sequence), [Viewer](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#viewer), [Scope](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#scope), [Technique](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#technique), [Pattern](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#pattern), [Recipe](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#recipe), [Case](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#case).

Each section and subsection opens with a glossary that names its terms and links each one to its definition in the package [Glossary](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md). A local glossary adds to the global glossary and to its parent section’s glossary, and a link to another glossary brings in that glossary’s terms. The numbered leaves make claims, give examples, or propose checks; they do not open further subsections.

This roundup is drawn from [Video know-how: craft](../Home.md), and its claims about cases keep that shelf’s source snapshot and review limits. Each link leads to the fuller explanation.

- 1 Give the piece a purpose that each scene can serve.
  - 1.0 **Section glossary.** Terms: [Premise](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#premise), [Direction](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#direction), [Setup](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#setup), [Payoff](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#payoff).
  - 1.1 State what the viewer should understand or feel by the end.
    - 1.1.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [global glossary](#root) and [direction glossary](#1.0). Terms: [Scene job](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#scene-job).
    - 1.1.1 Write the premise in one sentence that names the subject and what happens to it. Then give each scene a job that supports that sentence.
    - 1.1.2 For each scene, identify the complete image the viewer must read, the action that changes it, what arrives from the previous scene, and what continues into the next.
    - 1.1.3 When a page becomes a node in a graph, the move can show that the document belongs to a larger collection. Keep the page and make clear what the graph represents. The viewer needs that mapping to follow the camera move.
    - 1.1.4 Ask a first-time viewer to describe what the ending changed, and compare their account with the premise. [Source: direction and critique](../knowledge/Direction%20and%20critique.md).
  - 1.2 Vary the scenes while keeping their purpose clear.
    - 1.2.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [direction glossary](#1.0). Terms: [Variation](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#variation), [Consistency](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#consistency), [Motif](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#motif).
    - 1.2.1 Scenes can differ in composition and action. In each one, keep the claims accurate, the required text readable, and the consequences understandable.
    - 1.2.2 SLASH_MY’s documented direction moves from frustration through competence to elation, and its execution, design, writing, and navigation passages each use a different visual world. That is one way to answer its brief.
    - 1.2.3 The AX DAY reconstruction names the event in the opening request and holds back the finished video. SLASH_MY holds back its complete identity. Decide what to reveal from the question the opening raises.
    - 1.2.4 To review a decorative layer or a redundant scene, remove it from a comparison copy and check whether the premise becomes harder or easier to follow. [Sources: direction](../knowledge/Direction%20and%20critique.md), [the SLASH_MY case](../cases/SLASH_MY.md).
- 2 Give viewers enough time to read the title, labels, and result.
  - 2.0 **Section glossary.** Terms: [Visual hierarchy](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#visual-hierarchy), [Story beat](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#story-beat), [Settling](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#settling), [Reading time](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#reading-time).
  - 2.1 Give each story beat one primary change.
    - 2.1.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [attention glossary](#2.0). Terms: [Primary change](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#primary-change), [Supporting movement](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#supporting-movement), [Grouping](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#grouping).
    - 2.1.1 Compose a still that reads well on its own before planning how it enters, and decide what the viewer should notice first. A small flashing counter can pull attention away from a large title.
    - 2.1.2 Move a label with the object it describes. Keep the two visibly associated when objects sort, shrink, or change groups.
    - 2.1.3 A title reveal, a supporting line, a credit, and a highlight sweep each need a different role. Schedule them so the viewer can follow those roles in order. [Source: foundations](../knowledge/Foundations.md).
  - 2.2 Count reading time from the last required arrival.
    - 2.2.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [attention glossary](#2.0). Terms: [Entrance](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#entrance), [Stagger](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#stagger), [Complete state](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#complete-state).
    - 2.2.1 Entrance, settling, and reading time each take their own share of the shot’s time. A long shot can hold little reading time if its last word arrives just before the cut.
    - 2.2.2 For equal animation durations and stagger intervals, last completion equals start + duration + (element count − 1) × interval. Recalculate it when the wording changes.
    - 2.2.3 At delivery size, review the first complete phrase and the next event that competes with it. If the interval between them is too short, shorten the copy, reduce the stagger, or delay the next event.
    - 2.2.4 Keep the title, essential labels, and final result visible with the sound muted or motion reduced. Where a calmer or static version is required, an encoded video needs a separate version. A browser preference cannot change its frames. [Sources: foundations](../knowledge/Foundations.md), [motion character](../knowledge/Motion%20character.md).
  - 2.3 Keep chart values and product behavior accurate.
    - 2.3.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [global glossary](#root). Terms: [Observed value](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#observed-value), [Interpolation](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#interpolation), [Reference baseline](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#reference-baseline), [Simulation](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#simulation).
    - 2.3.1 In data animation, keep the units, reference baselines, labels, and object identity, and hold the endpoints long enough to compare them.
    - 2.3.2 Label generated in-between values when viewers could mistake them for observations. An eased progress display can imply that a process changed speed.
    - 2.3.3 A repair animation can explain how something works. A claim about the product needs evidence of how the product actually behaves, and any simulation or retiming has to be made clear. [Sources: foundations](../knowledge/Foundations.md), [make a deliverable](../maps/Make.md).
- 3 Build each component so its movement, form, text, and surface support the same event.
  - 3.0 **Section glossary.** Terms: [Component](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#component), [Path](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#path), [Motion spacing](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#motion-spacing), [Form](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#form), [Material](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#material), [Depth](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#depth).
  - 3.1 Choose the movement’s meaning before tuning its curve.
    - 3.1.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [component glossary](#3.0) and [settling definition](#2.0). Terms: [Easing](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#easing), [Recoil](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#recoil), [Follow-through](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#follow-through), [Stepped time](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#stepped-time).
    - 3.1.1 Decide whether the movement should suggest arrival, force, flexibility, procedure, or instability. Then coordinate path, motion spacing, contact, and response to match.
    - 3.1.2 Changing the easing changes progress along a path. Compare positions at equal time steps to see acceleration, check the largest excursion for cropping, and check the tail for whether it holds still enough to read.
    - 3.1.3 At the cited SLASH_MY snapshot, a 12 fps pain clock inside 60 fps output holds each state for five frames. All shutter samples see that held state, so the instability stays sharp.
    - 3.1.4 Keep that clock local to the defect. The repair can then use continuous movement while the rest of the piece stays smooth. [Sources: motion character](../knowledge/Motion%20character.md), [jitter to repair](../cases/Jitter%20to%20repair.md).
  - 3.2 Treat the title as an event with a readable result.
    - 3.2.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [component glossary](#3.0) and [reading time definition](#2.0). Terms: [Glyph](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#glyph), [Target geometry](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#target-geometry), [Compositing](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#compositing), [Headroom](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#headroom).
    - 3.2.1 Measure the actual font and wording before placing particle targets, masks, underlines, and supporting copy, and recompute those positions when the text changes.
    - 3.2.2 In the cited AX DAY reconstruction, the impact begins at 16 seconds and solid type is fully opaque at 17.1 seconds, while supporting information keeps arriving after that. Each line’s reading time starts at its own complete state.
    - 3.2.3 Layer order and effect duration affect readability. The source draws solid type over the particles, and its strongest glitch mostly ends before the viewer has to read the letters.
    - 3.2.4 A moving sheen needs brightness headroom on the letter faces. Check whether it travels across the surface or only enlarges a halo. [Source: typographic events](../knowledge/Typographic%20events.md).
  - 3.3 Inspect form before adding surface detail.
    - 3.3.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [component glossary](#3.0). Terms: [Occlusion](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#occlusion), [Aperture](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#aperture), [Grain](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#grain), [Matched stills](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#matched-stills).
    - 3.3.1 Check silhouette, proportion, joins, and placement at full size and at thumbnail size. Then inspect texture, light, occlusion, and inner walls.
    - 3.3.2 D-003 compared three changes: repairing the contour, moving grain behind the object, and adding a recessed aperture. John tentatively preferred the repaired contour, then questioned the form itself. The study produced stills and no rendered film.
    - 3.3.3 A cleaner join can support the object without making it worth using. If the object adds little to the premise, sketch another visual relationship before polishing its surface. [Source: form, material, and depth](../knowledge/Form%20material%20and%20depth.md).
- 4 Show the action and its result before naming the benefit.
  - 4.0 **Section glossary.** Terms: [Reveal](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#reveal), [Comparison](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#comparison), [Defect](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#defect), [Repair](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#repair), [Result](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#result).
  - 4.1 Prepare a reveal with a specific question.
    - 4.1.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [scene glossary](#4.0) and [setup and payoff definitions](#1.0). Terms: [Sync landmark](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#sync-landmark), [Continuity carrier](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#continuity-carrier).
    - 4.1.1 Establish what is missing and why its appearance matters. Keep a recognizable carrier through the preparation, and hold the result after it arrives.
    - 4.1.2 The AX DAY reconstruction’s particles share the title’s target geometry before the drop. The build prepares one particular result, and the solid letters and event facts then complete it.
    - 4.1.3 For an explainer, a quiet uncovering or an early result can work better than a long build. Decide what the viewer needs to discover before choosing the mechanism. [Source: meaningful reveal](../patterns/Meaningful%20reveal.md).
  - 4.2 Hold the reference conditions steady in a comparison.
    - 4.2.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [scene glossary](#4.0). Terms: [Endpoint](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#endpoint), [Reference condition](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#reference-condition), [Confound](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#confound).
    - 4.2.1 Name what the comparison is about: amount, position, correctness, speed, or appearance. Keep the labels, scale, units, and identity consistent with that question.
    - 4.2.2 Use paired endpoints for exact comparison, an overlay for alignment, or sequential movement to trace one object, and keep enough context to read the difference.
    - 4.2.3 When comparing sound treatments, match the listening level. Record which picture and sound properties changed, and keep uncertain responses in the record. [Source: focused comparison](../patterns/Focused%20comparison.md).
  - 4.3 Show what acts on the defect and what changes afterward.
    - 4.3.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [scene glossary](#4.0). Terms: [Mechanism](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#mechanism), [Payoff wording](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#payoff-wording).
    - 4.3.1 Keep enough order in the defective state that the viewer recognizes the system as it should work. A detached label or a stale connection may show the problem more clearly than jitter.
    - 4.3.2 Show the action that corrects the defect, stop the instability that belongs to it, and keep the same objects in the completed result.
    - 4.3.3 Hide the payoff wording in a review copy and ask whether the viewer can still identify the repaired relationship. Then restore the text and sound and check the full passage. [Source: defect to repair](../patterns/Defect%20to%20repair.md).
- 5 Connect the scenes through retained identity, developing motifs, and changes in density.
  - 5.0 **Section glossary.** Terms: [Continuity carrier](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#continuity-carrier), [Motif](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#motif), [Density](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#density), [Rest](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#rest).
  - 5.1 Decide what should survive the boundary.
    - 5.1.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [sequence glossary](#5.0). Terms: [Seam](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#seam), [Handoff](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#handoff), [Draw ownership](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#draw-ownership).
    - 5.1.1 Record the outgoing and incoming position, scale, orientation, crop, direction, and sound phrase. Keep only the relationships the next scene needs.
    - 5.1.2 In the cited SLASH_MY page transition, the written page becomes a graph node. The source keeps its content and geometry, hands draw ownership to the next scene, and combines a pullback with a deliberate cut to rest.
    - 5.1.3 Inspect frames before, on, and after the seam. Follow the page and listen across the cut, checking for duplication, replacement, or a camera reset. For a dense factual diagram, a fixed overview with an inset may work better. [Sources: continuity carriers](../patterns/Continuity%20carriers.md), [page to larger context](../cases/Page%20to%20larger%20context.md).
  - 5.2 Give each return a changed role.
    - 5.2.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [motif definition](#5.0). Terms: [Invariant](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#invariant), [Development](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#development).
    - 5.2.1 List the motif’s appearances. For each one, state what stays recognizable and what the viewer now knows.
    - 5.2.2 SLASH_MY uses the caret first as John’s cursor and later as part of its identity. Its strike answers a different complaint each time, and its musical hook changes role across sections.
    - 5.2.3 A recurring question, measurement, or viewpoint can make the same kind of connection. A short explanation may need only stable labels and clear state changes. [Source: motif development](../patterns/Motif%20development.md).
  - 5.3 Reduce activity where the viewer needs time.
    - 5.3.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [density and rest definitions](#5.0). Terms: [Concurrency](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#concurrency), [Foreground](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#foreground).
    - 5.3.1 Put words, movements, musical changes, and sound cues on one schedule. Look for collisions, and for long stretches that ask the viewer to do the same thing again and again.
    - 5.3.2 SLASH_MY’s calmer writing passage changes palette, camera behavior, reading task, and groove at once, and its page becomes the next scene’s subject. Copying only the pale palette would keep one of those changes and lose the rest.
    - 5.3.3 In the AX DAY reconstruction, the short gap before the drop serves anticipation and the longer title hold serves reading. Choose each interval for its task, and review the full rise, rest, and return. [Source: density and rest](../patterns/Density%20and%20rest.md).
- 6 Compose the score’s progression and give each sound a clear place in the mix.
  - 6.0 **Section glossary.** Terms: [Score](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#score), [Beat](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#beat), [Musicality](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#musicality), [Sound production](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#sound-production), [Cue](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#cue).
  - 6.1 Plan the musical relationships across scenes.
    - 6.1.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [sound glossary](#6.0). Terms: [Phrase](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#phrase), [Melody](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#melody), [Harmony](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#harmony), [Groove](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#groove), [Augmentation](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#augmentation).
    - 6.1.1 Give the phrase an arrival and space after it, and line that arrival up with the visual result. When narration carries the explanation, spoken meaning can set the timing.
    - 6.1.2 Develop recognizable material through rhythm, register, harmony, or arrangement. In the cited SLASH_MY score, the hook starts in the typing rhythm, changes with each section’s groove, slows in the writing passage, and comes back as the reveal’s lead.
    - 6.1.3 Listen to bass attacks and kicks together. Ducking can remove a bass attack that lands on a kick. Audition a timing change within the full phrase before deciding it helps. [Source: musical development](../knowledge/Musical%20development.md).
  - 6.2 Shape and separate the sounds that express the action.
    - 6.2.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [sound glossary](#6.0). Terms: [Envelope](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#envelope), [Timbre](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#timbre), [Masking](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#masking), [Ducking](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#ducking), [Send and return](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#send-and-return).
    - 6.2.1 Align the important audible attack with the intended visual sync landmark. A cue can start on time and still reach its body after the contact.
    - 6.2.2 Choose one foreground for each passage: speech, the musical hook, an action cue, or silence. Use level, spectral space, and routing to make that choice audible.
    - 6.2.3 Decide which tails survive a cut. A deliberate silent gap may require muting the wet return as well as the dry source, and a continuity handoff may keep the tail.
    - 6.2.4 Listen to the full mix, isolate one role to find the problem, then go back to the full mix. Compare versions at matched listening level. [Source: sound production](../knowledge/Sound%20production.md).
- 7 Build a workbench that reproduces the intended states and exposes delivery failures.
  - 7.0 **Section glossary.** Terms: [Representation](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#representation), [Deterministic rendering](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#deterministic-rendering), [Capture](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#capture), [Encoding](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#encoding), [Delivery](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#delivery).
  - 7.1 Choose representations and clocks for the required view.
    - 7.1.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [workbench glossary](#7.0). Terms: [Requested time](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#requested-time), [Seed](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#seed), [Bake](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#bake), [Sync landmark](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#sync-landmark).
    - 7.1.1 A flat image gives one view. A camera orbit that shows hidden surfaces needs more information, and text needs loaded fonts and measured bounds.
    - 7.1.2 Make evaluation at a requested time reproducible. When frames are requested out of order, a stateful simulation needs fixed stepping, checkpoints, or a bake.
    - 7.1.3 Record a shared event’s sync landmark and its units, then derive the visual and audio times from it. Trigger time can differ from visible arrival or audible attack. Reproducible frame state and byte-identical encoding are separate properties. [Source: production workbench](../knowledge/Production%20workbench.md).
  - 7.2 Write the boundary contract and inspect the actual file.
    - 7.2.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [workbench glossary](#7.0) and [handoff definitions](#5.1.0). Terms: [Coordinate space](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#coordinate-space), [Overlap interval](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#overlap-interval), [Decoded file](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#decoded-file).
    - 7.2.1 Before composing the scenes, state the outgoing and incoming geometry, coordinate space, overlap, draw ownership, and the sound that carries across the boundary. For a small project, a short boundary table can be enough.
    - 7.2.2 Inspect the first useful frame, the strongest treatment, the last required arrival, the boundaries, and the ending. Seek out of order as well as playing forward.
    - 7.2.3 Decode and play the delivered file at delivery size. Check frame count, streams, color, opening behavior, and sound hierarchy. A preview can run on different clocks from capture, or have its assets ready at different times.
    - 7.2.4 Adapt to the viewing medium. Vertical video needs stable captions and framing that suits the format. Pages the reader controls need to handle interruption and reversal, and newsletters and thumbnails need stills that work on their own. The upstream [Awesome AI Motion](https://github.com/gongnyang/awesome-ai-motion) collection’s medium map gives the general rule: choose the deliverable before carrying over a technique’s numbers, because time, navigation, available controls, screen size, and fallback behavior change what the same motion is for. Its seven deliverable routes pair each of recorded video, vertical feed, product intro, infographic, deck or site, newsletter, and thumbnail still with the adaptation that matters most for it. [Source: production workbench](../knowledge/Production%20workbench.md).
- 8 Use evidence that can answer the review question and keep each finding within its conditions.
  - 8.0 **Section glossary.** Terms: [Source inspection](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#source-inspection), [Rendered-frame inspection](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#rendered-frame-inspection), [Continuous playback](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#continuous-playback), [Listening](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#listening), [Human feedback](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#human-feedback), [Interpretation](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#interpretation).
  - 8.1 Match the inspection to the failure.
    - 8.1.0 **Subsection glossary.** Use the [evidence glossary](#8.0). Terms: [Symptom](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#symptom), [Candidate cause](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#candidate-cause), [Ablation](../../video-knowhow-code/Glossary.md#ablation).
    - 8.1.1 Describe the failure at a particular moment. Unreadable text may arrive late, move too long, crop badly, or compete with surface detail; choose a comparison that tells those possibilities apart.
    - 8.1.2 Use stills for clipped text and broken joins, boundary frames for spatial resets, playback for pacing and reading time, and listening for groove, timbre, and masking. Read the source to find mechanisms worth testing.
    - 8.1.3 After a local repair, replay the whole piece and check whether the change helps the premise, the scene’s task, and the sequence around it. [Source: diagnose](../maps/Diagnose.md).

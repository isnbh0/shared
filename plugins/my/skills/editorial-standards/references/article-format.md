# Article Format

Optional structure for a publication project. Use it only when the user requests this format or the current project already uses it. It does not apply to research notes, documentation, correspondence, or other one-off writing — for those, `editorial-standards.md` alone governs.

## Article structure

1. **Hero section** — title, author, date.
2. **Lead paragraph** — one-paragraph summary of the thesis.
3. **Analysis sections** — evidence-first prose under descriptive section headers.
4. **Data elements** — structured data supporting the analysis.
5. **Assessment** — explicit evaluation.
6. **Conclusions** — numbered takeaways, each with a lead sentence followed by supporting evidence.

## Table of contents

Each entry shows the title, a brief description, and a link to the full article. This is the index page, not part of any article.

## Precedence against the prose rules

Two places where this format and `editorial-standards.md` pull against each other, resolved:

**Lead paragraph vs. 4.4 (Lead with Evidence, Not Verdict).** No conflict once the level is right. The article opens with its thesis; evidence-first applies inside each analysis section, where the reasoning is what the reader is being walked through.

**Numbered takeaways vs. 4.3 (Earn Your Emphasis).** The lead sentence of each takeaway carries emphasis by position — it comes first and the evidence follows it. Bolding it as well doubles the device, which 4.3 rules out. Leave it unbolded unless the numbered list runs long enough that scanning genuinely needs the visual anchor.

## Terms this file does not define

"Data elements" and "Assessment" are load-bearing here but unspecified, and the boundary between "Assessment" and "Conclusions" is not drawn anywhere. If you are producing an article and cannot tell what belongs in each, ask rather than guessing.

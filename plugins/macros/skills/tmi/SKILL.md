Honor every skill explicitly activated in the user's request exactly once. If another activated skill is not yet loaded and the host provides a skill-loading mechanism, load it through that mechanism. Do not reload an active skill.

Review the target text and flag anything a future reader doesn't need. Session residue is one source: content that only makes sense if you were in the room when it was written. Epistemic padding is another: content that answers a doubt no reader would have.

## Signals

- Defensive justification for decisions ("Why we didn't X")
- Arguments against alternatives not presented in the artifact
- Speculative claims about the future ("As models improve...")
- Implementation backstory leading to a decision, rather than the decision itself
- Content that restates what other sections already imply
- Over-hedging: softening qualifiers on claims already stated at the strength their evidence supports
- Boundary caveats: a sentence naming what a finding does not establish or has not verified ("remains to be verified", "확인되지 않음"), including as "rather than" or "not X but Y" contrasts. Keep one only if a careful reader would otherwise conclude something false, the gap changes their next step, or it names the step that would close it. A caveat that points to a specific place a reader would plausibly assume was covered, and says why it was not, is earned. Cut caveats that only negate the claim's own scope words or hedges, or that could follow any claim in the text.

If the text was actually produced by the ongoing conversation, use that history as your reference point for what served the discussion vs. what belongs in the artifact.

## Output

Report what's TMI and why — like a review. Only directly edit if the user's message includes explicit instructions to do so.
